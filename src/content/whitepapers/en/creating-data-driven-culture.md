## What is a Data-Driven Culture?

Data-driven cultures make data an imperative part of everyone’s daily workflow, and these organizations use data to execute and prioritize initiatives. A culture that is data-driven also instills trust and commitment in all members of the organization as they can collaborate fluidly with transparency based on shared metrics with data at their fingertips.

This whitepaper will give you the keys to building a culture where everyone collaborates based on data, helping to make smarter, faster decisions. From your business leaders to analysts to your information workers – everyone is included in ensuring a data-driven culture is successful.

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Leaders need to shift the organization&#8217;s mindset to begin embracing data and alter how everyone approaches decision-making. <li>Set aside time for training yourself as an executive to learn new data skills</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading whitepapers__headline-dividers" id="chapter-6">Trust</h2>


<div class="whitepapers__chapter-breadcrumbs d-block d-md-none mt-3 mb-4"><ul class="list-inline list-inline--piped"><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-1" class="">Chapter 1</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-2" class="">Chapter 2</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-3" class="">Chapter 3</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-4" class="">Chapter 4</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-5" class="">Chapter 5</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-6" class="text-secondary">Chapter 6</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-7" class="">Chapter 7</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-8" class="">Chapter 8</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-9" class="">Chapter 9</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-10" class="">Chapter 10</a></li></ul></div>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most employees and management in organizations want to know that they can trust the analytics they see. This is why it is important that everyone in your organization is basing their decisions on the same metrics. Therefore, having a single source of truth is so important. Without it you can have employees pulling metrics from different sources and risking inconsistencies, resulting in less trust in the data.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When there is one place for your users to find answers and consistent data, you eliminate the need for people to begin searching for different systems. This also makes it easier for the data administrators, so they only need to maintain data in one location.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Take some of the following steps to build out an environment that serves as a single source of truth that the whole organization can draw from:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Bring together your data administrators to inform them of the initiative and the value.</li>

<li>Work cross-departmentally to identify the data and views your end users need.</li>

<li>Cleanse the data so that it is easy for your end users to digest.</li>

<li>Choose a tool to build your single view in.</li>

<li>Ensure that everyone in your organization can access the data.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading whitepapers__headline-dividers" id="chapter-8">Metrics (That Matter)</h2>


<div class="whitepapers__chapter-breadcrumbs d-block d-md-none mt-3 mb-4"><ul class="list-inline list-inline--piped"><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-1" class="">Chapter 1</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-2" class="">Chapter 2</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-3" class="">Chapter 3</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-4" class="">Chapter 4</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-5" class="">Chapter 5</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-6" class="">Chapter 6</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-7" class="">Chapter 7</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-8" class="text-secondary">Chapter 8</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-9" class="">Chapter 9</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-10" class="">Chapter 10</a></li></ul></div>

<figure class="wp-block-pullquote"><blockquote><p>Trying to force people to conform their work to preestablished numerical goals tends to stifle innovation and creativity—valuable qualities in most settings. And it almost inevitably leads to a valuation of short-term goals over long-term purposes.</p><cite>Jerry Z. Muller, The Tyranny of Metrics</cite></blockquote></figure>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The first step in measuring business improvements is making sure that the key performance indicators (KPIs) are the ones that matter and are tied to your organizational goals. After all what you are tracking is what your efforts are working towards to improve, right?</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">First, ensure you understand a key performance indicator (KPI). KPIs are measurements that tell you how a process is performing so appropriate business decisions can be made. Organizations should have organizational-wide KPIs, such as sales targets, department-wide KPIs, such as the number of inbound leads marketing is generating, and performance-level KPIs, such as employee satisfaction, customer satisfaction, and customer retention.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So how do you know if you are tracking the right metrics or not? These steps will help you focus your KPIs and help you narrow down the ones that matter most:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Choose the right KPIs to measure success.</li>

<li>Research and look at historical data to set realistic goals.</li>

<li>Focus on a few important KPIs rather than too many.</li>

<li>Identify new technology needed for the organization to access and analyze data.</li>

<li>Assign data administrators responsibility for ensuring accurate data and providing the proper access to the organization.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading whitepapers__headline-dividers" id="chapter-9">Training</h2>


<div class="whitepapers__chapter-breadcrumbs d-block d-md-none mt-3 mb-4"><ul class="list-inline list-inline--piped"><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-1" class="">Chapter 1</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-2" class="">Chapter 2</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-3" class="">Chapter 3</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-4" class="">Chapter 4</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-5" class="">Chapter 5</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-6" class="">Chapter 6</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-7" class="">Chapter 7</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-8" class="">Chapter 8</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-9" class="text-secondary">Chapter 9</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-10" class="">Chapter 10</a></li></ul></div>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Data can only make an impact if it&#8217;s incorporated in the decision-making process:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Training employees on “how” to get to data is just the beginning</li>

<li>How to turn that data into “actionable insight” is the real goal
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>What tools do you currently have or need to acquire?</li>

<li>Is data readily available? REST APIs, OData, direct connections?</li>

<li>Does my team understand data visualizations?</li>

<li>Do we hire a data scientist or build from within?</li>

<li>Can we do effective Data Storytelling?</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Find key business stakeholders with a visibility and data mindset, give them data literacy training, and empower them to promote a Data-Driven culture in your organization.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading whitepapers__headline-dividers" id="chapter-10">How Embedded Analytics Can Foster a Data-Driven Culture</h2>


<div class="whitepapers__chapter-breadcrumbs d-block d-md-none mt-3 mb-4"><ul class="list-inline list-inline--piped"><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-1" class="">Chapter 1</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-2" class="">Chapter 2</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-3" class="">Chapter 3</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-4" class="">Chapter 4</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-5" class="">Chapter 5</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-6" class="">Chapter 6</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-7" class="">Chapter 7</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-8" class="">Chapter 8</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-9" class="">Chapter 9</a></li><li class="list-inline-item"><a href="#chapter-10" class="text-secondary">Chapter 10</a></li></ul></div>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So, what is embedded analytics, and how does it tie back to creating a data-driven culture?</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In contrast to traditional BI, which requires users to leave their workflow applications to look at data insights in a separate set of tools, <a href="https://www.revealbi.io/embedded-analytics" target="_blank" rel="noopener">embedded analytics</a> lets users view data visualizations or dashboards in context—while in the application itself. Bringing data right into the user’s workflow without any disruption. And in return, delivering more value.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Introducing data into a user’s daily workflow makes it easier for people to see and act on insights and help program them to constantly think analytically.</p>
